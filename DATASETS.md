# Datasets

One section per dataset, written before analysis and kept current. This file
is generated by `scripts/build_datasets_md.py` from `research/datasets/*.md`;
edit those files, not this one.

## Rules that apply to every gauge

**What a reading is.** Every value is the height of the water surface above
a fixed mark at the gauge, called the gauge zero. It is not the depth of the
river. Gauge zeros differ from place to place, so levels cannot be compared
between gauges, and readings can be negative. Lobith (cm above Dutch sea
level datum, NAP) and the two Colorado reservoirs (ft above sea level on the
Hoover Dam datum) are absolute heights; all others are local.

**Quality labels.** Each daily value carries one label, taken from the
publisher's own flags where they exist:
- `measured`: a reading the publisher has not flagged. For the Army Corps
  this means "not screened," not "verified."
- `provisional`: raw or not yet approved. Recent months at most gauges.
- `estimated`: filled, interpolated or reconstructed by the publisher, or by
  our documented rule (Kratie 1975 to 1979).

**Daily value.** Most gauges give a daily mean. Army Corps gauges give one
reading a day (usually 8 a.m.). Paris and Melun give the daily maximum.
Lobith is a single morning reading before 1979 and a daily mean after. Each
gauge's JSON says which.

**What we never do.** No gap filling, no smoothing of the stored data, no
unit conversion. Gaps stay gaps. Any datum adjustment is applied at build
time from a documented offset and disclosed on the page.

**Partial days.** A day is kept only once it has ended. Each fetch records
the date of its last real download in `data/raw/.downloaded/<script>.txt`,
and any reading dated on or after that date is dropped, because a mean of
that day's readings covers only part of the day. The rule applies to every
gauge, including one-reading-a-day gauges, so the record always ends on a
finished day. With the October 15, 2026 cache it removes October 15 at
Manaus, Obidos, Tabatinga, Ladario, Asuncion and the four Mekong gauges.
Added October 17, 2026, from the live build, where Manaus and Rosario
ended on a part-day mean.

**Sources that need permission are left out.** A river is not shown when
its publisher's terms clearly require permission to republish. As of
October 19, 2026 this is the Mekong: the Mekong River Commission's
Non-commercial Data Use Licence (version C, 2005) allows no redistribution
of data or derived products without MRC's written permission. All four
Mekong level gauges and all four flow series are left out of the page
(`excluded` in config/rivers.json). The fetch scripts and processed files
stay, so the river can return if MRC grants permission. Sources that state
no license at all (INA, DMH-DINAC, Hub'Eau, BfG hyd1d) are shown, with
credit, and are published without asking the publishers first (decision
October 20, 2026). Where a dataset section below says "clear before
publishing," this decision replaces that step.

**Build-time changes (config/rivers.json).** Applied when building the page;
the processed files stay as published.
- Bismarck (Missouri): 4.24 ft added to every reading before October 1,
  1934, when USGS lowered the gauge zero by that amount (USGS Water-Supply
  Paper 1309).
- Chiang Saen (Mekong): 0.62 m added to every reading before December 16,
  1993. The station moved on December 15, 1993, and the published series
  jumps exactly 0.62 m overnight (1.88 m to 2.50 m); Cochrane et al. (2014,
  Hydrology and Earth System Sciences) give the same 0.62 m correction.
- Lake Mead and Lake Powell: record low and high count only from when each
  lake first filled (Lake Mead July 1, 1941; Lake Powell June 22, 1980, per
  Reclamation). Earlier readings stay in the chart, shaded "Lake filling."
- Bourke (Darling): the page starts on January 1, 1972. The record has part
  of 1895, then nothing until mid-1972.
- Seine: Paris only. Melun was dropped (83% of its readings never validated,
  no data 1984 to 2005).
- Paris (Seine) is published in mm and shown in meters.
- Thin vertical lines mark documented breaks without changing data: Bismarck
  October 1, 1934; Chiang Saen December 15, 1993 (station moved, earlier
  readings adjusted); Louisville 1923 (low-water level steps up, cause not
  documented).
- Kaub shows reference lines at 78 cm and 40 cm.

**What the page shows for each gauge.** Latest reading and date; lowest and
highest daily reading with dates over the whole shown record; the first
year; how many calendar years are missing more than 5% of days (first and
last years count only days inside the record); and the share of days with a
reading for each year. Displayed values are rounded half away from zero:
whole cm, 0.1 ft, 0.01 m. Stored daily means are rounded half to even (the
pandas default) to the publisher's precision, so a mean of exactly 74.5 cm is
stored as 74.

**Daily-value rules found in the independent tie-out (October 15, 2026).**
- Mekong (MRC): isolated 0.00 daily values more than 1 m from both
  neighboring days are dropped as placeholders (Chiang Saen 2, Vientiane 5,
  Pakse 1, Kratie 2). A 0.00 reading inside a day that also has a reading more
  than 1 m away is dropped before averaging (Kratie January 1, 2006 had 8.47
  m and 0.00; the mean of 4.24 m had wrongly become the record low).
- Lobith: when the once-a-day series has an unchecked reading beside a
  checked one on the same day, the checked one is used (January 1, 1979 and
  1981). A day is estimated when more than half its readings carry an
  interpolation code (10, 20 or 25); 17 days from 2006 to 2011 are. When a day
  is both unchecked and mostly interpolated, provisional wins (31 days in
  August to October 2026).
- Canada (ECCC real-time): a day gets a daily mean only if it has readings
  in at least 20 of its 24 hours; partial days are left out. Days run on local
  standard time (UTC-8).
- Manaus telemetry: where ANA returns the same timestamp twice with different
  values (2,435 timestamps from December 2014 to November 2022), the first one
  is kept.
- ANA gauges (Ladario and others): in a month where ANA publishes a
  daily-mean row, a day left blank in that row is left out, even if one of the
  07:00 or 17:00 readings exists (22 days at Ladario, 2010 to 2014). Months
  with no daily-mean row use the mean of the 07:00 and 17:00 readings.
- Paris: the reading of -1.25 m on September 15, 1946 is the low point of a
  three-day dip that Hub'Eau flags as good; it is kept and noted on the page.

**The 12-month average.** The viz draws a trailing 365-day mean of the daily
values, computed in the browser. It is drawn only on days when at least 300
of the previous 365 days have a reading.

**Completeness.** For every gauge the viz shows, by calendar year, the share
of days with a reading, computed in the browser from the same data. Each
gauge JSON also carries `coverage_by_decade` with the shares measured,
provisional and estimated.

## Natural Earth (map geometry)

**What it is.** Public domain vector map data: land outlines (1:50m), lakes
(1:50m) and river centerlines (1:10m), version 5.1.2 from the
`nvkelso/natural-earth-vector` GitHub repository.
**Known quirks.** Rivers are split into numbered segments (`rivernum`). The
Paraguay River is labeled as part of the "Paraná" (segment 36), so we split
it at latitude 27.25 S, near the confluence. The upper Mekong is labeled
"Lancang." Lines are simplified for the page (Douglas-Peucker, 0.02 degree
tolerance for rivers), so gauges can sit slightly off the drawn line.
**License.** Public domain; credit appreciated.

## Flow (discharge) and satellite levels

Researched October 17, 2026, after a reviewer suggested DAHITI and the
UW-Madison SAGE discharge database. **Built October 17, 2026** for 37
gauges (34 beside a level gauge, 3 flow-only) from the publishers' APIs;
see "As built" below. SAGE/RivDIS, GRDC-Caravan, GEOGLOWS and DAHITI are
documented here but **not on the page**. Facts were checked by real requests on October 17,
2026 where no account was needed; full notes with URLs are kept with the
research.

Three kinds of data, never mixed on one axis:
- **Measured level** (the rest of this file set): a gauge reading above a
  local zero.
- **Flow (discharge):** how much water passes, almost always computed from
  level through a rating curve. Measured flow and modeled flow are kept
  apart.
- **Satellite level:** height above a model of sea level at the point where
  a satellite track crosses the river, every 10 to 35 days.

### Rules that apply to every flow series

**What a discharge value is.** Discharge is how much water passes the gauge,
in cubic meters per second (m³/s) or cubic feet per second (cfs; 1 cfs is
0.0283 m³/s). Almost no publisher measures it every day. Crews measure flow
a few times a year, and those measurements fix a rating curve: a rule that
turns a level into a flow. Each daily discharge is the day's level put
through the rating in force at the time. So discharge is only as good as
the level and the rating.

**Limits that apply to every rated series.**
- Rating curves are refit as new measurements come in, and riverbeds change.
  Publishers apply shifts between measurements and revise recent months,
  sometimes years. Old values may have been computed with a rating that was
  later replaced.
- The highest and lowest flows are often above or below any flow ever
  measured at the gauge, so the rating is extended beyond its data. Record
  floods and record lows are the least certain values in the series.
- Ice changes the level for a given flow. In winter, publishers estimate
  flow by other means (Bismarck, Shelley).
- Backwater from a dam, a tide or a larger river raises the level without a
  change in flow. At those gauges a simple rating does not work, and the
  publisher uses other methods or publishes no discharge (Manaus, Mission,
  Ladário for most years).
- Where flow comes from a nearby station, the page will say which station and
  how far away, and the two series are not combined.


### Flow at the gauges we already show

One block per publisher. Researched for all 43 gauges.

**PEGELONLINE and BfG (Rhine at Maxau, Kaub, Ruhrort; Elbe at Dresden,
Magdeburg, Wittenberge).**

*Discharge.* PEGELONLINE publishes discharge (Q) every 15 minutes for all six
gauges, computed by the waterways administration from the level with a
discharge table. It keeps only the last 31 days, and the values are raw.
The BfG yearbook (dgj-daten.bafg.de) has checked daily discharge as PDF
tables, one year per page, for about 1998 to 2025, and summary values back
to about 1900; Ruhrort's yearbook file has level only. We found no
machine-readable long daily record that we can republish: the BfG data
service provides it on request, and GRDC holds it but forbids republishing.
No license is stated on the yearbook server. Until then these six gauges would show
no discharge history. Limits: rating curves are revised as the riverbed
changes, which on the Rhine and Elbe has been large (see the level notes on
bed erosion); BfG notes that discharge at Ruhrort cannot be computed below
120 cm right now.


**Rijkswaterstaat (Rhine at Lobith).**

*Discharge.* Same API and location as level, with Grootheid `Q` (m³/s).
One reading a day at 08:00 from 1901, then 10-minute readings in recent
years. Years before 1901 return nothing, so discharge starts 35 years after
level. Values carry the same Kwaliteitswaardecode and Statuswaarde as level;
even 1901 values are marked "Ongecontroleerd" (unchecked), so status alone
does not show which old values were checked. Lobith discharge is computed
from the level with a rating that Rijkswaterstaat updates from regular
measurements. Limits: flood peaks are above most measured flows, so they
are the least certain values. License CC0 1.0.


**GKD Bayern (Danube at Pfelling and Hofkirchen).**

*Discharge.* Same download center as level, with the topic
`fluesse.abfluss` and daily means (`tmw`). Pfelling runs from November 1,
1925, and Hofkirchen from November 1, 1900, both earlier than their level
records. Each day has a mean, and from later years a maximum and minimum.
Values through 2025 are "Geprueft" (checked); 2026 values are "Rohdaten"
(raw) and will change. Discharge is computed from the level with a rating
curve that GKD updates. Limits: ratings are revised as the riverbed
changes; high-flood values are extended beyond measured flows. License CC BY 4.0, credit "Datenquelle: Bayerisches
Landesamt für Umwelt, www.lfu.bayern.de".


**eHYD (Danube at Kienstock).**

*Discharge.* `file=5` of the same eHYD service (Q-Tagesmittel, daily mean,
m³/s), January 1, 1978 to December 31, 2023, with one gap. Computed from
level with a rating ("Pegelschlüssel"), per the station master data, from
1976. Like level, it is yearbook data and ends in 2023. Footnotes say many
months from 1996 to 2023 were computed from filled-in levels. License CC BY
4.0, credit "Datenquelle: ehyd.gv.at".


**Hub'Eau (Seine at Paris).**

*Discharge.* `obs_elab` with `grandeur_hydro_elab=QmnJ` (daily mean flow), in
liters per second. Same historical stations as level: F700000109 (1900 to
1965), F700000110 (1966 to 1973), F700000111 (1974 to 1989), F700000102
(1990 on), and the dedicated flow station F700000103 at Austerlitz (2006 on).
Each value has a status (raw, pre-validated, validated), a method
(calculated, expert-reviewed, reconstructed) and a qualification (good,
doubtful). Before 1955, about three in four values are qualified "doubtful."
Recent days are raw. Limits: Paris flow is regulated by upstream lake
reservoirs, which hold back winter floods and raise summer low flows, so
recent flows are not natural (dates to be added from a source). License not stated.


**USGS NWIS (St. Louis, Memphis, Louisville, Bismarck, Hermann; Helena to
1977).**

*Discharge.* Parameter 00060, daily mean (statistic 00003), cfs. St. Louis
from 1861, Louisville from 1928, Bismarck from 1927, Hermann from 1928,
Memphis from 1933 with no values from about 1995 to 2014. USGS flags each day
A (approved) or P (provisional), with "e" for estimated. About 10% of
Bismarck's days are estimated, mostly winter days with ice. USGS computes
discharge from the level with a rating curve, adjusted by "shifts" after
each field measurement (usually every 6 to 8 weeks), and approves a year of
data some months after it ends. Cincinnati has only 15-minute discharge from
an index-velocity meter since October 2024. For Cairo the plan is Olmsted
(03612600, about 17 river miles upstream, from 2013); for Cincinnati,
Markland Dam (03277200, about 61 river miles downstream, from 1970); for
Helena after 1977, Memphis (about 72 river miles upstream). Limits:
on the Mississippi the same level can carry different flows on a rising and
falling river, and the earliest St. Louis values rest on few field
measurements (how USGS built the 1861 to 1930s record is not yet sourced). The legacy service is being retired; the new
API (api.waterdata.usgs.gov) returns the same values. Public domain, credit
U.S. Geological Survey.


**USACE CWMS (Vicksburg).**

*Discharge.* `MVK:Vicksburg.Flow.Inst.1Day.0.DCP-rev`, cfs, one value a day
from January 2, 1901, to September 13, 2026, the same family and reading
time as the level series. Quality code 0 (not screened) throughout, so the
Corps file gives no approval status. USGS 07289000 has daily means at
Vicksburg from 2008 and could check the overlap. Limits: same as level
(several gauge sites before 1977), plus the rating questions above. Public
domain, credit U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.


**RISE (Lake Powell, Lake Mead).**

*Discharge.* A lake has no river flow at the gauge, so the plan is to show release: the
daily average flow let through the dam. Lake Powell (Glen Canyon Dam) item
4315, from March 14, 1963; Lake Mead (Hoover Dam) item 6125, from April 1,
1934; both cfs, updated daily, provisional like the elevation series. The
release is set by dam operators under the river's operating rules, so it
reflects decisions, not just weather. Reclamation also publishes a computed
inflow for Lake Powell (item 511), not a gauged one; it swings widely from
day to day. RISE has no inflow for Lake Mead. USGS gauges just below Glen
Canyon Dam (Lees Ferry, 09380000) measure the released water independently.
Public domain, credit U.S. Bureau of Reclamation.


**ECCC Water Survey of Canada (Fraser at Shelley and Hope; Mission).**

*Discharge.* Same HYDAT and real-time services as level, field `DISCHARGE`,
m³/s. Shelley from 1950 (longer than its level record, which starts in 2011),
Hope from 1912. HYDAT is approved and runs to 2025; later days are real-time
and provisional. Each day can carry a symbol: B (ice conditions), E
(estimated), A (partial day). At Shelley about a third of days in recent
decades carry the ice symbol, because winter ice changes the level for a
given flow. Mission is tidal and has discharge only for 1965 to the early
1990s and scattered days after, so for Mission the plan is to show Hope's flow, 68 km
upstream, and say that tributaries in between add to it. Open Government
Licence - Canada.


**ANA HidroWeb (Tabatinga, Manacapuru, Óbidos; Ladário 1964 to 1973).**

*Discharge.* Same `HidroSerieHistorica` service as level, with
`tipoDados=3`, m³/s. Tabatinga from 1995, Manacapuru from 1972, Óbidos from
1968. Checked ("consistido") values run through 2023; later months are raw
and end in early to mid 2026. Each day has a status: real, estimated or
doubtful. ANA publishes no discharge at Manaus, where the Rio Negro is
backed up by the Solimões, and Ladário has discharge only for 1964 to
1973, so these two gauges would show none. Limits: on these large, flat rivers the
same level gives different flows on the rise and the fall, so ratings are
uncertain, especially at record highs and lows. License: none stated by ANA.


**INA (Paraná at Corrientes, Paraná, Rosario; Posadas to 2017).**

*Discharge.* Same a5 API as level, variable 40 (daily mean flow), m³/s:
Corrientes from 1904, Paraná and Rosario only from July 2015, Posadas 1970
to September 2017. INA labels the per-reading series as computed from a
rating curve ("curva de gasto"). Posadas sits in the Yacyretá reservoir; for
recent years the plan would be Yacyretá's inflow or outflow, 85 km downstream.
No per-value quality flags. Asunción has no discharge series; the nearest
is the Paraguay at Puerto Bermejo, 203 km downstream, which lags by months.
License: none stated.


**BoM Water Data Online (Swan Hill, Euston, Bourke; Albury via Doctors
Point).**

*Discharge.* Same KiWIS service, parameter "Water Course Discharge," series
`DMQaQc.Merged.DailyMean.24HR`, m³/s. Swan Hill from 1974, Euston from 1975,
Bourke mostly from 1972. Albury's own discharge record stops in 1952; the plan is
Doctors Point (409017), 4 km downstream, from 1929. Quality codes: A (best
available), B (compromised), C (estimate), E (unknown). More than half of
Doctors Point's days are E. The Murray is regulated by Hume Dam and weirs,
so flows reflect releases. CC BY 4.0, credit Bureau of Meteorology and the
station owner.


**MRC (Chiang Saen, Pakse, Kratie; Vientiane to 2006).**

*Discharge.* Same time-series API, parameter "Discharge" ("Calculated daily
discharge"), m³/s: Chiang Saen from 1960, Pakse from 1923, Kratie from 1924.
Vientiane's series ends in 2006; Nong Khai, 13 km downstream, runs to 2026.
All series carry approval level "Working," not final. Kratie repeats each
daily value hourly; we keep one per day. Limits: none documented by MRC in the series
metadata; Kratie and Pakse carry notes we have not read. Upstream dams
change dry-season flows (dates to be sourced). MRC Non-commercial
Data Use Licence, not open. Not shown on the page from October 19, 2026,
because the license requires MRC's written permission to republish.

**On the page.** Each gauge with flow shows a dial (lowest to highest daily
value on record) that moves with the playback date, and a separate daily
flow chart with its own axis, 12-month average (same 300-of-365 rule) and

### Global Runoff Data Centre, GRDC (BfG, Koblenz): not usable on the page

**What it is.** The main global archive of measured daily and monthly river
flow, supplied by national services. Flow only, no water level.

**License and attribution.** The downloaded values may not be redistributed
"in part or total ... to the general public (incl. distribution via
Internet)," and are "released only for research purposes." "Analysis and
further processing ... as well as the use of statistical data products is
permitted" with credit. The terms say nothing about charts; a chart from
which a reader can read the monthly or yearly values is very likely
redistribution in part. We use GRDC only as a check, unless grdc@bafg.de
agrees in writing. The GRDC-Caravan extension below is the open exception:
a subset that national services allowed GRDC to share under CC BY 4.0.

**Where nothing open exists.** China (no historical downloads; data treated
as confidential), India (Indus and Ganga-Brahmaputra basin data classified),
Pakistan (daily press releases only), the Niger Basin Authority (no public
access policy found), SIEREM (refers requests to national services) and
Russia's water cadastre (moved into a closed government system). For those
rivers the open options are old monthly records in SAGE/RivDIS, modeled
flow, or satellite level.

### Global River Discharge Database (SAGE, UW-Madison), mostly RivDIS v1.1

**What it is.** Monthly mean river flow in cubic meters per second at about
3,100 listed stations. Each file has one row per year with 12 monthly means
and an annual mean. Our target stations all come from RivDIS (Vorosmarty,
Fekete and Tucker), which was typed in from UNESCO's published yearbooks of
national flow records. This is flow, not water level.

**Where it comes from.** One tab-delimited text file per station, no login:
`https://sage.nelson.wisc.edu/riverdata/station_table_formated.php?pointid=<PointID>`.
Station list: `keysearch.php?numfiles=50&startnum=<N>` (75 pages). Station
reliability flags: RivDIS `STATION.DAT` at ORNL DAAC (open). Files are a few
kilobytes each.

**Version and vintage.** The site is the 1999 CPEP data set (Coe and
Olejniczak), built on "RivDis2.0," USGS and Brazil's DNAEE (now ANEEL).
RivDIS v1.1 was released in 1998 (doi:10.3334/ORNLDAAC/199). No update date,
no version on the site, no sign of upkeep. Values are frozen.

**Coverage.** Station records run from 3 to 116 years. Median end year 1984;
nearly every record ends before 2000. Our gauges: Kinshasa 1903 to 1983,
Brazzaville 1984 to 2002, Koulikoro 1907 to 1990, Niamey 1929 to 1991, Aswan
1869 to 1984, Dongola 1912 to 1984, Volgograd 1879 to 1984, Komsomolsk 1933
to 1984, Farakka 1949 to 1973, Keban 1937 to 1972. RivDIS does not say which
months were estimated by the national services. River flow is not measured
directly most days: it is computed from water level through a rating curve
that is checked now and then by field measurement.

**Changes over time.** Dams change what the numbers mean: Aswan after the
High Dam (1964 to 1970), Volgograd after 1958, Keban after 1974. The Aswan
series after 1964 is controlled release, not natural flow.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None known.

**Missing data.** -9999 marks a missing month. A year with any missing month
has a -9999 annual mean. Missing years have no row.

**Revisions.** None; the archive is frozen. ArcticGRO's newer Roshydromet
data differ from SAGE by more than 5% in a few months (Yenisei June 1983 is
10,700 in SAGE and 106,873 in ArcticGRO), which points to typing errors
rather than revisions.

**Units and rounding.** m3/s, three or four significant figures, as typed
from the yearbooks. The annual mean is SAGE's own average of the 12 months.

**Known quirks.**
- Kinshasa April 1903 reads 3,280 between 32,410 and 34,970: a dropped digit.
- Khartoum (Blue Nile) is labeled 1976 to 2046. The years are wrong. Do not use.
- RivDIS flags Staritsa (Volga) and Paksay (Ganges) "Bogus Discharge," and
  Brazzaville "Second station in the same cell."
- PointIDs are not unique across sources: 75 PointIDs belong to two stations
  (one RivDIS, one ANEEL). Always check the station name in the file header.
- The site says 3,681 stations; the index lists 3,125 entries.
- Some RivDIS entries have a blank Source field (Komsomolsk on the Amur).

**Uncertainty.** RivDIS gives no error bars; its guide cites 5% to 10% as
typical for river gauging, "but the actual accuracy depends significantly on
local conditions." Early records, ice periods and floods are less certain.

**License and attribution.** No license on SAGE. CPEP: "The data are freely
available." NASA's RivDIS record points to NASA's open data policy. The
values came from UNESCO's open publication, not from a GRDC download, so
GRDC's no-redistribution terms do not apply to them in our reading. Credit:
"Vorosmarty, C.J., B.M. Fekete and B.A. Tucker (1998), Global River
Discharge, 1807-1991, V1.1 (RivDIS), ORNL DAAC; via the SAGE Global River
Discharge Database, University of Wisconsin-Madison." Ask SAGE for written
confirmation before launch.

### Discharge Dataset (Arctic Great Rivers Observatory, ArcticGRO)

**What it is.** Daily mean river flow in m3/s for the Ob at Salekhard, the
Yenisei at Igarka and the Lena at Kyusyur, measured by Roshydromet, Russia's
weather and water service. Each row is one day with a flag.

**Where it comes from.** https://arcticgreatrivers.org/discharge/ . The
xlsx files are embedded in the page as base64 (the page is about 32 MB);
we decode them. File names carry the version, for example
`Ob_Salekhard_Version_20260810.xlsx` (about 850 KB). No login.

**Version and vintage.** Version 20260810. Each update gets a new date code
and the old one is archived. Updates replace provisional values with
certified ones.

**Coverage.** 1936 to December 2025 (Ob) or January 2026 (Yenisei, Lena).
Certified ("A") values: Ob to 2008, Yenisei to 2005, Lena to 2021. Later
values are blank-flagged or provisional ("P"). Russian provisional values
are estimated by the Arctic and Antarctic Research Institute from water
level, ice thickness and past rating curves; winter values are redone after
the ice breaks up.

**Changes over time.** Dams upstream (Novosibirsk on the Ob; Krasnoyarsk and
others on the Yenisei and Angara) change the seasonal pattern from the 1950s
on.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** Rows with "NA" for value and flag: Ob 60 days (2000),
Yenisei 2,502 days (mostly 1963 to 1979), Lena 43 days (1962). Lena also has
146 rows with no value in late 2024 to 2025.

**Revisions.** Yes: provisional and blank-flagged values get replaced. Pin
the version code and refresh in full.

**Units and rounding.** m3/s, three significant figures as published.

**Known quirks.** The blank flag is not explained in the metadata; we treat
it as provisional. Lena has both A and P values in 2012 to 2021.

**Uncertainty.** None published. Winter flow under ice is the least certain.

**License and attribution.** "ArcticGRO data are free to use by anyone for
any purpose. We only ask that you credit ArcticGRO." Credit: "The Arctic
Great Rivers Observatory. 2026. Discharge Dataset, Version 20260810. Data
from Roshydromet."

### GRDC-Caravan extension (Farber et al. 2025, GRDC)

**What it is.** Daily river flow for 5,356 gauges in 25 countries whose
national services let GRDC share data openly. We use the Orange at
Vioolsdrif (South Africa) and the upper Zambezi at Katima Mulilo (Namibia).
Measured by the national services.

**Where it comes from.** Zenodo record 15349031 (version 0.6, May 6, 2025),
`GRDC_Caravan_extension_csv.zip` (8.8 GB). Single files can be pulled from
the zip with HTTP range requests. No login.

**Version and vintage.** v0.6. Fixed release.

**Coverage.** 1950 to 2023 (Caravan cuts every record at 1950). Vioolsdrif
1950-01-02 to 2023-05-19, 52 complete years. Katima Mulilo to 2021-06-21, 58
complete years.

**Changes over time.** The Orange is heavily dammed (Gariep 1971, Vanderkloof
1977) and partly diverted.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** Rounding acts like masking at low
flow (next item).

**Missing data.** Empty streamflow cells. Not filled.

**Revisions.** None within a version.

**Units and rounding.** Flow is stored as mm per day over the basin area,
rounded to 0.01 mm/day. Convert with area (km2) x mm/day / 86.4 = m3/s. At
Vioolsdrif one step is about 91 m3/s, and 11,684 days read exactly zero
(below about 45 m3/s). Use monthly means and say so. At Katima Mulilo one
step is about 39 m3/s.

**Known quirks.** The area used is Caravan's delineated basin (786,037 km2 at
Vioolsdrif), not the official one.

**Uncertainty.** None published.

**License and attribution.** CC BY 4.0. Credit "GRDC-Caravan (Farber et al.,
2025), data from the Department of Water and Sanitation, South Africa, and
the Ministry of Agriculture, Water and Land Reform, Namibia, via the Global
Runoff Data Centre."

### DAHITI satellite river levels (DGFI-TUM, Technical University of Munich)

**What it is.** Water surface height at "virtual stations," the points where a
satellite's path crosses a river. Each row is one satellite pass: a date, a
height in meters above a model of sea level, and the publisher's error
estimate for that pass. It is a height above a sea-level model, not a gauge
reading and not a depth.

**Where it comes from.** https://dahiti.dgfi.tum.de . Data through the DAHITI
API version 2 (`https://dahiti.dgfi.tum.de/api/v2/download-water-level/`) in
JSON, CSV, ASCII or NetCDF. Needs a free account and an API key; without a key
the API returns HTTP 403. Station lists and station info pages are public.
Each series is small (100 to 700 rows).

**Version and vintage.** Each file names a software version; our stations are
on version 8.0. The publisher describes version 6.0 on its product page and
does not publish a change log. Most stations update within weeks of each pass;
our stations end between late August and early October 2026. We pin the
download date and keep the raw files.

**Coverage.** 2002 to 2026 at best, 2008 to 2026 on most of our rivers, 2016
to 2026 on the Orange and Yenisei. A few stations reach back to 1995. Every
value is a measurement from one satellite pass; the publisher does not fill
gaps. Several passes or missions can be blended into one series with a Kalman
filter, which weights each measurement by its error. One value every 10 days
on Jason-series tracks (2002 or 2008 on), every 27 days on Sentinel-3 (2016
on), every 35 days on Envisat, ERS-2 and SARAL (1995 to 2016).

**Changes over time.** The satellite changes over time. On the main 10-day
track, Jason-1 (2002 to 2009) was followed by Jason-2 (2008), Jason-3 and
Sentinel-6A. Envisat records break in October 2010 when its orbit changed, and
SARAL records on the same track end in 2016. Some stations jump from one
mission to another with a gap of years and a shift in where the track crosses
the river. The publisher corrects for known differences between missions, but
small offsets can remain at each change.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** Passes judged bad are removed, not
flagged. Removal uses height limits, echo strength (also used to drop ice), and
a test for points that do not fit a flat water surface. Limits are set by hand
for each station. Nothing is top-coded or rounded beyond millimeters.

**Missing data.** Gaps are simply absent rows. Short gaps are passes that were
removed. Long gaps, up to several years, come from mission changes. A listed
start date can be a stray early point: Brahmaputra 318 is listed from 1995 but
starts for real in 2008.

**Revisions.** Whole series are reprocessed when the software changes. We
refresh the whole series and record the download date and software version.

**Units and rounding.** Meters, to the millimeter (API JSON carries more
digits). Real accuracy on rivers is about 10 cm to 1 m, so the published
precision is far finer than the real accuracy.

**Known quirks.** The height is above a sea-level model (EIGEN-6C4 in version
6.0; version 8.0 not stated). It differs from any gauge zero by an unknown
fixed amount, and from the next station upstream by the river's slope, so only
the rise and fall at one station is meaningful. Station names repeat ("Niger,
River" is 167 stations); use the ID. Spellings vary (Zambesi, Zambezi,
Zambeze). Winter values on Siberian rivers may be affected by ice.

**Uncertainty.** The publisher says accuracy ranges from a few centimeters on
large lakes to a few decimeters on small rivers. Its method paper found errors
of 8 to 114 cm on Amazon rivers compared with gauges. The per-pass error in the
files is much smaller (often under 3 cm, sometimes 0) and should not be shown
as an error bar on its own.

**License and attribution.** CC BY-NC 4.0 (Attribution-NonCommercial; the
registration page's text says "Attribution 4.0" but links to the NonCommercial
license). The terms of use allow free use only by private persons, schools and
universities, and nonprofits, and forbid commercial use. Confirm in writing
with DGFI-TUM (christian.schwatke@tum.de) before publishing. Credit: "DAHITI,
DGFI-TUM," and cite Schwatke et al. (2015), Hydrology and Earth System Sciences
19, 4345-4364, doi:10.5194/hess-19-4345-2015.

## European river levels

Written 2026-10-15 for the RiverLevels build. One section per source. Fetch
scripts are in `scripts/fetch/`; each gauge's processed JSON repeats the key
notes, a sourced `measurement_history`, and short `public_notes`.

Gauges covered here: rhine-maxau, rhine-kaub, rhine-ruhrort, elbe-dresden,
elbe-magdeburg, elbe-wittenberge (hyd1d + PEGELONLINE); rhine-lobith
(Rijkswaterstaat); danube-hofkirchen, danube-pfelling (GKD Bayern);
danube-kienstock (eHYD); seine-paris, seine-melun (Hub'Eau).

A general trap for all of them: a gauge reading is the water surface measured
against a fixed mark (the gauge zero), usually set below the riverbed. It is
not the depth of the river. Gauge zeros differ from place to place, so levels
cannot be compared between gauges, and a reading can go below zero.

### hyd1d gauging data (Bundesanstalt für Gewässerkunde, BfG)

**What it is.** A data frame shipped with the BfG's hyd1d R package. Each row
is one gauge and one day: `gauging_station`, `date`, and `w`, the daily mean
water level in cm above the gauge zero. It holds 70 federal gauges on the
Rhine and Elbe.

**Where it comes from.** https://hyd1d.bafg.de/downloads/df.gauging_data_latest.RDS,
an R `.RDS` file of about 2.2 MB, no login. Read with `pyreadr`. Package page:
https://cran.r-project.org/package=hyd1d. Help page for the data:
https://hyd1d.bafg.de/reference/df.gauging_data.html. The code repository moved
from GitHub to https://gitlab.opencode.de/bafg-bund/hyd1d in version 0.5.5.

**Version and vintage.** Our copy has HTTP Last-Modified Tue, 11 Aug 2026
04:43 GMT and runs to 2026-08-10 for every gauge. The package (0.5.5, published
2026-08-19) adds one validated year per release; the NEWS file lists validated
imports for 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025. Between releases a daily job is meant to
append recent days from PEGELONLINE, but this file stopped at August 10, 2026.

**Coverage.** 1960-01-01 to 2026-08-10 for most gauges; Kaub starts
1969-11-01. Our six gauges have no missing days inside that span. The help page
says data from 1960-01-01 to 2025-12-31 "are validated and were queried from
(WISKI7)-database". Days after 2025-12-31 are "continuously collected from"
PEGELONLINE and "are not officially validated." The repository script that
builds those days takes the plain mean of the 15-minute raw values for the day
and rounds it to whole cm (data-raw/daily_pegelonline2gauging_data.R). There
is no gap filling described. We mark 1960 to 2025 as measured and 2026 as
provisional.

**Changes over time.** Gauge zeros are set by the waterway administration.
The values are relative to the gauge zero in force at the time, and the file
carries no record of past zero changes. Our neighbor comparison
(`scripts/fetch/hyd1d_stepscan.py`) compares each gauge with two to four
neighbors on the same river and flags lasting shifts of 15 cm or more. It found
no gauge-zero step at Maxau, Kaub, Ruhrort, Dresden, Magdeburg-Strombrücke or
Wittenberge. As a control it does find the known step of about 100 cm in
November 1979 when Köln is compared with Düsseldorf; the same jump shows at
Bonn and Rees, so Köln, Bonn and Rees should all be left out or corrected.
Shifts that do appear at Magdeburg (20 to 50 cm against Barby and Rothensee)
follow river flow rather than a single date. Maxau's relation to its neighbors
drifts by 10 to 20 cm around 2001 to 2004 and then returns, which looks like
riverbed change, not a datum change.

Riverbed erosion changes what a level means over time:

- Lower Rhine: a federal waterway working group found the official equivalent
  low-water level (GlW) fell by up to 8 cm from 2002 to 2012 at gauges from
  Mainz to Düsseldorf, and that the low-water level at Kaub fell about 45 cm
  since the 1930s at the same flow, even though the bed there is largely rock
  (https://izw.baw.de/publikationen/vzb_dokumente_oeffentlich/0/Frei-fliessende-Rhein_2014.pdf).
  From GlW 2012 to GlW 2022, Düsseldorf and Ruhrort fell 6 cm and Emmerich
  10 cm (https://www.ccr-zkr.org/12030100-en.html). The Ruhrort to Emmerich
  reach also sank from coal and salt mining
  (https://izw.baw.de/publikationen/kolloquien/0/vortrag_luellau.pdf). We did
  not find a sourced rate in cm per year for these gauges.
- Elbe: the bed between Mühlberg and the Saale mouth still drops by up to
  about 2 cm a year in places, and sediment has been added since the
  mid-1990s to slow it
  (https://www.gesamtkonzept-elbe.de/Webs/Projektseite/GkElbe2020/DE/Projekte/Erosionsstrecke/Erosionsstrecke.html).
  The BfG gives up to about 2 cm a year and about 2 m from 1898 to 2004 at the
  worst spots, including near Magdeburg (https://doi.bafg.de/KLIWAS/2014/Kliwas_Binnenelbe.pdf).
  The fastest lowering at gauges from Dresden to Magdeburg, about 3 cm a year,
  came around 1890 to 1904 (https://izw.baw.de/publikationen.php?file=mitteilungsblaetter/0/mb74_Glazik.pdf).

So a record low today and a record low in the 1960s are not the same flow.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None found.

**Missing data.** No missing-value code. No empty values for our six gauges.
The file ends 2026-08-10 and the PEGELONLINE window starts 31 days before
each download, so August 11 to September 14, 2026 is missing. We leave it as a
gap.

**Revisions.** Yes. Raw recent days are replaced each year when the validated
year is imported. We refresh the whole file.

**Units and rounding.** cm above gauge zero. Most values are whole cm; some
years (1994 to 2016) carry decimals. Provisional days are rounded to whole cm.

**Known quirks.** Raw 2026 values are not screened: Schnackenburg (not one of
our gauges) shows 3,361 cm on 2026-06-23, which is impossible. Our six gauges
show no such spikes in 2026 (largest day-to-day change 143 cm at Ruhrort
during the February flood). A HEAD request to the RDS returns HTTP 400; GET
works.

**Uncertainty.** The help page gives no error bars.

**License and attribution.** The package code is GPL (>= 2). We found no data
license for the gauging data. We checked the help page, the hyd1d website
(its imprint link returned 404 on 2026-10-15), the CRAN page, the GitLab
project (no LICENSE file; the API reports `license: None`), the README,
inst/CITATION (which cites Weber et al. 2023, Ecohydrology & Hydrobiology,
doi:10.1016/j.ecohyd.2023.04.001) and REFERENCES.bib ("Provided by
Bundesanstalt für Gewässerkunde, Koblenz, Germany"). No Zenodo or DOI record

### PEGELONLINE REST API v2 (Wasserstraßen- und Schifffahrtsverwaltung des Bundes, WSV)

**What it is.** Live 15-minute water levels for federal waterway gauges. Each
measurement is a timestamp and a value in cm above gauge zero. Station
metadata give coordinates, river km, and the gauge zero.

**Where it comes from.** https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/webservices/rest-api/v2,
no key. We call `/stations/{number}.json?includeTimeseries=true` and
`/stations/{number}/W/measurements.json?start=P31D`. Docs:
https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/webservice/dokuRestapi.

**Version and vintage.** API v2; breaking changes get a new URL version. Data
are live. Each of our refreshes saves its pull under a dated file name and all
saved pulls are merged, so the recent record grows with each refresh.

**Coverage.** Only the last 31 days ("Es können Daten für die letzten 31 Tage
bezogen werden"). Our first pull covers 2026-09-14 to 2026-10-15. We keep only
MEZ days (UTC+1, the German hydrological day) with at least 90 of 96
quarter-hour values, so the partial first and last days drop out. All values
are marked provisional.

**Changes over time.** The `gaugeZero` for most WSV gauges is listed as valid
from 2019-11-01, the date the height system switched from DHHN92 to DHHN2016
(https://www.hamburg-port-authority.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Gewaesserkundliche_Information_2020.pdf).
That moved the gauge zero in m NHN by a few millimeters to centimeters but did
not change the readings. Maxau is the exception (valid from 2017-07-18; reason
not found). The help page still says DHHN92, which looks out of date.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** Missing quarter hours are simply absent.

**Revisions.** The 31-day window is raw data and is not revised in the API.
The checked values appear later in hyd1d.

**Units and rounding.** cm, whole numbers. We round daily means to whole cm,
as hyd1d does.

**Known quirks.** Station numbers are not the same as hyd1d names
(Duisburg-Ruhrort 2770010 is RUHRORT in hyd1d). The PEGELONLINE station
remark for Ruhrort says discharge cannot be computed below 120 cm right now.

**Uncertainty.** "Bei den heruntergeladenen Daten handelt es sich um
ungeprüfte Rohdaten ... können Ausreißer, Messfehler oder andere
Unregelmäßigkeiten enthalten." (https://pegelonline.wsv.de/gast/hilfe)

**License and attribution.** DL-DE->Zero-2.0 per the terms of use (Stand
21.05.2024, https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/gast/nutzungsbedingungen). No
attribution required; we credit "WSV, PEGELONLINE" anyway.

### Waterwebservices / DDL API (Rijkswaterstaat)

**What it is.** Dutch national water data. For Lobith (`lobith.bovenrijn.tolkamer`)
we pull water height (`WATHTE`, ProcesType `meting`) in cm relative to NAP,
the national datum. Each reading carries a quality code
(Kwaliteitswaardecode) and a status (Statuswaarde).

**Where it comes from.** POST to
https://ddapi20-waterwebservices.rijkswaterstaat.nl/ONLINEWAARNEMINGENSERVICES/OphalenWaarnemingen,
no key, one request per calendar year (161 requests). Docs:
https://rijkswaterstaatdata.nl/waterdata/ and
https://github.com/Rijkswaterstaat/WaterWebservices. Limit: 160,000 readings
per request (about 3 years of 10-minute data). The raw JSON is about 7 to
14 MB per modern year; we store it gzipped (6 MB total).

**Version and vintage.** The current services went live on 5 December 2025.
Data run to the time of the request.

**Coverage.** 1866-01-01 to 2026-10-14, every day present. The API also
returns two readings from January 30 and 31, 1865, which we leave out. Lobith
levels "worden al gemeten sinds 1866" (https://edepot.wur.nl/505109). What a
day means changes:

- 1866 to 1934: one staff-gauge reading a day, usually at 08:40.
- 1935 to 1985: one reading a day from a float gauge chart, read by eye
  (08:40, then 08:00 from the 1950s).
- 1979 to 1987: unchecked hourly chart readings exist alongside the checked
  daily reading; we use the checked daily one. 1986 has only the hourly ones.
- 1987 onward: automatic float gauge, hourly values until 2010, every 10
  minutes from 2011. We average them to a daily mean.

So `daily_stat` is "mixed": 43,800 single-reading days and 14,900 averaged
days. A single morning reading misses peaks inside the day.

**Changes over time.** NAP is checked by precise leveling every 25 to 40
years (https://www.rijkswaterstaat.nl/zakelijk/open-data/normaal-amsterdams-peil/geschiedenis-nap).
A 2005 benchmark correction of about 2 cm in the western Netherlands appears
only in secondary documents; we did not verify it in an RWS source. Gauge
relocations at Lobith: not found. The Rhine bed erosion described above
affects Lobith too: its official equivalent low-water level is 733 cm + NAP
for 2023 to 2031 (https://www.ccr-zkr.org/12030100-en.html).

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** 999999999 (or 999999) is the fill code when data are
missing, and quality code 99 marks a gap. We dropped 10 such readings. We drop
days with fewer than 90% of the usual readings for their series (one day:
today's partial day).

**Revisions.** Yes. Status moves from Ongecontroleerd (unchecked) to
Gecontroleerd (checked) to Definitief after periodic checks, including
re-leveling of the station height. No stated delay. We refresh every year.

**Units and rounding.** cm relative to NAP, whole cm per reading; daily means
are rounded to 0.1 cm.

**Known quirks.** Quality codes from the Aquo list
(https://rijkswaterstaatdata.nl/publish/pages/223004/aquo_kwaliteitsoordeelcodes.xlsx):
00 normal; 03 larger spread; 10, 20, 25 interpolated in space and/or time;
50 implausible; 90 deviating value approved after validation; 99 gap. Only 00,
25 and 99 occur at Lobith (7,297 readings with 25). We mark a day estimated if
more than half its readings are 10, 20 or 25 (no day reaches that), and
provisional if any reading is Ongecontroleerd. Provisional days: most of 1986,
parts of 1979 to 1987, and recent months. Two series often overlap in the same
year; the script picks one per day.

**Uncertainty.** Not stated.

**License and attribution.** CC0: "Op de inhoud van de WaterWebservices is de
Creative Commons zero verklaring (CC0) van toepassing." Credit
"Rijkswaterstaat" anyway.

### Gewässerkundlicher Dienst Bayern, GKD (Bayerisches Landesamt für Umwelt)

**What it is.** Bavarian state hydrology portal. Daily values per gauge:
date, daily mean, daily max, daily min, and a check status (Prüfstatus). We use
the daily mean water level in cm above gauge zero for Hofkirchen (10088003) and
Pfelling (10078000) on the Danube. Both gauges are run by the federal waterway
office (WSA Donau MDK).

**Where it comes from.** https://www.gkd.bayern.de, no login and no email
needed. The script POSTs to `/de/downloadcenter/enqueue_download` with
`zr=gesamt` (whole period) and `wertart=tmw` (daily values) and an AJAX header,
then polls the returned token link until it redirects to a zip on
download.gkd.bayern.de (20 to 40 seconds). The zip has one CSV for everything
up to the end of last year, one for this year, one for the current month, and
one for the current, unfinished day (which we skip).

**Version and vintage.** Live database; each file header prints its query
time. Downloaded 2026-10-15.

**Coverage.** Hofkirchen 1925-11-01 to 2026-10-14, Pfelling 1927-04-04 to
2026-10-14, with no missing days. Values through 2025-12-31 are "Geprueft"
(checked); from 2026-01-01 they are "Rohdaten" (raw). Both gauges switch on
the same date, which suggests a yearly check. Daily values are means of all
readings in the day ("Tagesmittelwerte aus allen über einen Tag erfassten
Messwerten").

**Changes over time.** The file states only the current gauge zero (Hofkirchen
299.58 m NHN, Pfelling 308.12 m NHN, both DHHN2016), not past ones. Comparing
the two gauges, the gap between them widens by about 20 cm over 1964 to 1966
at similar flows; we could not tell which gauge changed or why, and flag it.
Barrages upstream (Straubing, Geisling) and riverbed change could affect the
level; dates and effects not found. Hofkirchen sits on the free-flowing stretch
between Straubing and Vilshofen.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** None in our two series. No fill codes seen.

**Revisions.** Raw 2026 values will be replaced when checked. Refresh the
whole series.

**Units and rounding.** cm, published with two decimals but whole numbers in
practice. The download page says ISO 8859-1; the files we got are UTF-8 with
a byte-order mark and decimal commas.

**Known quirks.** "Der Wasserstand entspricht nicht der Wassertiefe! Er
bezieht sich auf den Pegelnullpunkt, der unterhalb der Gewässersohle liegt."
Individual date ranges in the web form are limited to 5 years; "Gesamtzeitraum"
avoids that.

**Uncertainty.** "Rohdaten können Ausreißer bzw. fehlerhafte Werte enthalten."
No error bars given.

**License and attribution.** CC BY 4.0
(https://www.gkd.bayern.de/de/impressum). Required credit: "Datenquelle:
Bayerisches Landesamt für Umwelt, www.lfu.bayern.de".

### eHYD (Hydrographischer Dienst Österreich, BMLUK)

**What it is.** Austria's published hydrology archive. For Kienstock on the
Danube (HZB 207357, run by viadonau) we use "W-Tagesmittel": daily mean water
level in cm above gauge zero.

**Where it comes from.** https://ehyd.gv.at/services/MessstellenExtraData/owf?id=207357&file=2
(daily mean level, a semicolon text file in Latin-1) and `file=1` (station

No login.

**Version and vintage.** eHYD holds history only "bis zum aktuell
veröffentlichten Jahrbuch"
(https://www.bmluk.gv.at/themen/wasser/wasser-oesterreich/hydrographie/der-weg-zu-den-daten/Datenbestand_Bezug.html).
The yearbook runs years behind: the 2019 yearbook is dated November 2023.

**Coverage.** 1976-01-01 to 2023-12-31, no missing days. The file ends with a
row dated 2024-01-01 reading "Lücke" (gap). All values come from the checked
yearbook; there is no per-value flag, so all are marked measured.

**Changes over time.** From the station master data: gauge zero 194.00 m above
the Adriatic datum since 1974-01-01 with no change listed; staff gauge read by
hand from 1974; float recorder with paper chart 1976 to 1997, digitized from
1997; pressure sensor with logger from 2002; remote transmission from 2009;
station coordinates updated in 2010 (about 115 m); river km recalculated in
2018. The Altenwörth plant downstream (1976) and Melk plant upstream (1982)
may affect levels; effect not found.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** "Lücke" marks a gap. One at the end of the file.

**Revisions.** Yearbook values are final. New years arrive when a yearbook is
published.

**Units and rounding.** Whole cm.

**Known quirks.** Footnotes say discharge in many months from 1996 to 2023 was
computed "nach ergänzten Wasserständen" (from filled-in water levels), so some
level values were filled, but the level series does not say which days. We
keep them as measured and say so.

**Uncertainty.** Not stated.

**License and attribution.** CC BY 4.0 under Open Government Data Austria, per
the INSPIRE metadata record
(https://inspire-geoportal.ec.europa.eu/srv/api/records/55e494dd-e0af-4806-95d6-86e30dde0504).
We did not find this text on ehyd.gv.at itself. Required credit: "Datenquelle:
ehyd.gv.at", linked to https://ehyd.gv.at.

### Hub'Eau hydrométrie (Office français de la biodiversité / Schapi, data from PHyC)

**What it is.** French national hydrometry API. The `obs_elab` endpoint gives
elaborated daily series. We use HIXnJ, the daily maximum of instantaneous water
height, for Paris-Austerlitz and Melun on the Seine. Each row has the value, a
status, a method and a quality code.

**Where it comes from.** https://hubeau.eaufrance.fr/api/v2/hydrometrie/obs_elab
and `/referentiel/stations`, no key. Docs:
https://hubeau.eaufrance.fr/page/api-hydrometrie. Page size up to 20,000 with
cursor paging.

**Version and vintage.** API v2.0.1. HIXnJ was added 19/06/2025. Elaborated
data were recalculated and reloaded on 18/03/2024.

**Coverage.** Paris-Austerlitz is stitched from four station codes at the same
site, each used only inside its own window, with no overlapping days:
F700000109 (1900 to 1965), F700000110 (1966 to 1973), F700000111 (1974 to
1989), F700000102 (1990 on). All four list the same zero, 25.92 m. Codes 109,
110 and 102 give altimetric system 3 (NGF-IGN69); 111 gives 0 (unknown) with
the same altitude. F700000102 and the flow station F700000103 share 7,501 days
since 2006 with a median difference of 0 mm, which supports one reference. The
code switches fall on January 1, and the API has no value for December 31 and
January 1 at each switch (1965/66, 1973/74, 1989/90), so those two days are
gaps. We applied no
offset. Melun (F447000101) runs 1900 to 2026 but has no values from 1984-01-01
to 2005-10-01.

Status and method by period:

- Paris 1900 to 1989: validated; method "Mesurée" or "Calculée" (measured).
- Paris 1990 to 2019: validated, method "Expertisée" (edited by a
  hydrologist), which we count as measured.
- Paris 2020 on: mostly raw or pre-validated (provisional). Since 2020
  F700000102 is a backup gauge with unvalidated data.
- Melun before 1984: "Donnée corrigée" (corrected, not validated), so
  provisional. This is 83% of the Melun record.

Before 1966 the Paris values come from an observer reading at 7 am, more often
in floods, so the "daily maximum" is often one reading.

**Changes over time.** Reservoirs upstream change low and high water at Paris:
Pannecière (1949), Seine (1966), Marne (1974), Aube (1990). The Suresnes dam
downstream holds the level up until about 3 to 3.3 m at Austerlitz. Melun is
"fortement influencé" by the Vives Eaux dam, especially in the 2016 flood while
it was being rebuilt. Melun's current reference (36.01 m) is valid from
2004-10-01; older references are not listed.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** Missing days are absent. See gaps above.

**Revisions.** Yes; status moves from brute (4) to corrigée (8) to
pré-validée (12) to validée (16) (Sandre list 510), and the 2024 reload shows
whole histories can be recalculated. Refresh everything.

**Units and rounding.** Millimeters ("mm pour les hauteurs d'eau"). We keep mm.
Dates are UTC.

**Known quirks.** Method codes (Sandre list 512,
https://api.sandre.eaufrance.fr/referentiels/v1/nsa/512.json): 0 Mesuré,
4 Reconstitué, 8 Calculé, 10 Expertisé, 12 Interpolation, 14 Estimé,
16 Valeur forcée. We count 0, 8 and 10 as measured, the rest as estimated (none
occur in our data). Quality codes (list 515): 12 Douteuse (doubtful),
16 Non qualifiée, 20 Bonne, 30 Estimée. Doubtful values are kept and counted
in the gauge notes (17 at Paris, 31 at Melun).

**Uncertainty.** Station quality class for the pre-1966 Paris code is 12
(uncertain). No error bars.

**License and attribution.** Not stated. The Hub'Eau pages, the API spec
(`license: {}`), the data.gouv.fr record and HydroPortail's legal notice give
no license. French public data usually fall under Licence Ouverte / Etalab 2.0,
but we could not confirm it for this API. **Clear before publishing.** Credit
"Hub'Eau / HydroPortail".

Four publishers supply the North American gauges: the U.S. Army Corps of

### USACE Corps Water Management System, CWMS (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers)

**What it is.** River stage (water height above a fixed gauge zero) for
seven gauges: Mississippi at St. Louis, Memphis, Helena and Vicksburg; Ohio
at Louisville (McAlpine Dam lower gauge) and Cairo; Missouri at Hermann.
Each row is one reading per day, not a daily mean.

**Where it comes from.** CWMS Data API (CDA),
`https://cwms-data.usace.army.mil/cwms-data/timeseries`, JSON with header
`Accept: application/json;version=2`. No login. Series used:

| Gauge | Office | Series |
|---|---|---|
| mississippi-stlouis | MVS | `St Louis-Mississippi.Stage.Inst.~1Day.0.MVDhist-rev` |
| mississippi-memphis | MVS | `Memphis-Mississippi.Stage.Inst.~1Day.0.MVDhist-rev` |
| mississippi-helena | MVS | `Helena-Mississippi.Stage.Inst.~1Day.0.MVDhist-rev` |
| mississippi-vicksburg | MVK | `Vicksburg.Stage.Inst.1Day.0.DCP-rev` |
| ohio-louisville | LRL | `McAlpine-MLPK2.Stage-Tailwater.Inst.1Day.0.LPMS-comp` |
| ohio-cairo | MVS | `Cairo-Ohio.Stage.Inst.~1Day.0.MVDhist-rev` |
| missouri-hermann | MVS | `Hermann-Missouri.Stage.Inst.~1Day.0.datman-rev` |

Names were confirmed with the catalog endpoint (`/catalog/TIMESERIES`).
Coordinates and gauge zero come from `/locations/<name>`. Requests go one
decade at a time (about 1 second each, 16 to 18 per gauge). The 2020s chunk
for a 160-year gauge is about 0.2 MB of JSON.

**Version and vintage.** No version number. "rev" in a series name means
the district's revised (edited) copy. The public CDA lags the internal
database: on October 15, 2026, the MVS historical files ended September 11,
2026, Louisville September 24, Hermann October 3, and Vicksburg October 14.

**Coverage.** St. Louis 1861, Cairo 1858, Louisville 1875, Helena 1871,
Memphis 1885, Vicksburg 1901, Hermann 1922 (all to 2026). We did not find
a document for Cairo's 1858 start; the earliest NWS entries are an 1871 low
and an 1882 crest. Nearly every
calendar day has a value from the first full decade on (see
`coverage_by_decade` in each JSON). Hermann's 1920s decade is 37% because
the record starts in 1922. CWMS does not say which values are direct
readings and which were filled. Nearly every value carries quality code 0
(never screened), so the file cannot tell us. Vicksburg has 48 values
flagged as replaced (interpolated or graphically estimated, 2011 to 2019,
for example a smooth run from August to November 2019) and Hermann 5; these
are marked `estimated`.

**Changes over time.** Reading time drifts within the record: St. Louis has
8,357 readings stamped 07:59 instead of 08:00; Helena 4,273 at 08:09. These
are clock-label differences, not different statistics. Vicksburg and
Louisville are stamped at 12:00 UTC (6 a.m. Central Standard Time and 7 a.m.
Eastern Standard Time). Gauge moves, from USGS station histories:
St. Louis read a nonrecording gauge 0.4 mile downstream until May 5, 1934,
then a recorder, at the same datum. Memphis moved about 1.3 miles
downstream (1932 to 1934) at the same datum, and USGS gives a correction of
0.3 ft per 10 ft of stage to match the old Beale Street gauge. Vicksburg
used at least six sites from 1871 to 1977, all on one datum but on a
sloping river; in 1927 the canal gauge read about 2 ft above the bridge
gauge. Vicksburg's median level drops about 10 ft from the 1920s to the
1930s and 1940s; site changes and channel changes may both play a part, and
the Corps file does not say which gauge each year uses. Hermann read a
gauge 480 ft downstream at a datum 0.07 ft lower until September 26, 1930.
Louisville combined several gauges before October 1939 and in 1943 to 1946
(USGS). Louisville's yearly minimum also rises from about 3 to 5 ft before
1923 to about 9 ft from 1923 on; we found no document that explains it.
The Louisville project was Locks and Dam 41 until 1960, when it was renamed
McAlpine; the present dam was built in the 1960s. None of these moves are
adjusted in the data. Each gauge's `measurement_history` has the dates and
sources.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None found. Helena has one
99999 fill value; it is dropped.

**Missing data.** Missing days are simply absent. Louisville has 133 null
values and Vicksburg 5; dropped. CWMS also flags missing or rejected values
in the quality code; none appear in these series.

**Revisions.** The Corps edits recent data after review. We refresh the
whole series on each `--refresh`; there is no pinned vintage. Recent
duplicate readings (March to June 2024, a second reading at 09:00 next to
the 08:00 reading at St. Louis, Memphis and Cairo) look like a load artifact
around the daylight saving change; we keep the 08:00 reading.

**Units and rounding.** Feet above gauge zero. CWMS stores SI and converts,
so values come back as 12.979999999999999; we round to 0.01 ft, the
finest precision in the files.

**Known quirks.**
- The MVS location records for Memphis and Helena have coordinates 0, 0.
  The Vicksburg District (MVK) records for the same gauges have real
  coordinates and gauge zeros, which we use.
- The Louisville series (McAlpine-MLPK2) has no coordinates; we use the LRL
  record McAlpineLWR, "Ohio River at Louisville, KY", which matches USGS
  03294500.
- Gauge zeros are listed in different datums by different offices (NGVD29
  at Memphis, Vicksburg, Louisville and Hermann; NAVD88 at St. Louis,
  Helena and Cairo). The zero itself has not moved because of this; only
  the label of its elevation differs.
- Hermann has two copies; MVDhist-rev stops in December 2020, so we use
  datman-rev.
- USGS extension: where a USGS gauge reports the same zero, we append USGS
  instantaneous readings at the CWMS reading hour after the CWMS record ends,
  marked `provisional`. We check the datum on the overlap: St. Louis
  (07010000, 33 days added, median difference 0.00 ft), Cairo
  (370000089094501, a Corps gauge relayed by USGS, 33 days, 0.00 ft) and
  Hermann (06934500, 8 days, 0.00 ft) pass. Louisville (03294500) differs
  by a median 0.06 ft at 12:00 UTC and is not extended. Memphis (07032000)
  and Vicksburg (07289000) returned no recent gage height, and Helena has
  no USGS gauge.

**Uncertainty.** We found no Corps error bars for stage. Each day has
one reading, so it can miss a crest or trough later that day. How early
readings were taken is in each gauge's `measurement_history`.

**License and attribution.** U.S. federal government work, public domain.
Credit: U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (and U.S. Geological Survey for the
extended days).

### Reclamation Information Sharing Environment, RISE (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation)

**What it is.** Daily reservoir surface elevation for Lake Powell (Glen
Canyon Dam) and Lake Mead (Hoover Dam), in feet above sea level. Each row
is one instantaneous daily value.

**Where it comes from.** RISE API, `https://data.usbr.gov/rise/api/result`
(JSON:API, header `Accept: application/vnd.api+json`), item 508 (Powell,
source database `uchdb2`, Upper Colorado) and item 6123 (Mead, `lchdb2`,
Lower Colorado). No login. Pages of 10,000 rows take about 7 seconds each;
3 pages for Powell, 4 for Mead. The `order=ASC` parameter is ignored; rows
come newest first. Coordinates come from the RISE location record.

**Version and vintage.** Updated daily (catalog `updateFrequency: daily`).
Powell runs to 2026-10-13 and Mead to 2026-10-13 as downloaded.

**Coverage.** Powell 1963-12-28 on, Mead 1935-02-02 on, both with a value
on every day (share 1.0 in every decade). Both records begin while the lake
was filling: Mead's first value is 670.0 ft, more than 500 ft below full
pool. RISE does not say which values are direct readings.

**Changes over time.** Not documented in RISE. We found no record of how
or where Reclamation measures either lake's elevation, or of instrument
changes. Glen Canyon Dam began storing water upstream of Mead in March 1963,
which changed Mead's inflow.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** None in the period of record.

**Revisions.** The catalog disclaimer reads: "Data are provisional and
subject to revision unless otherwise noted." The per-value `status` field is
empty for every row, so RISE gives no row-level approval. Nearly all rows
carry an update date, which looks like bulk reloads, so RISE cannot show
which past values were revised. Our rule: values from October 1, 2025 on
(the current water year, plus the previous one until six months after it
ends) are `provisional`; earlier values `measured`. That puts the 2026
record lows (Powell 3,516.62 ft on September 15, 2026; Mead 1,037.60 ft on
October 9, 2026) in the provisional set.

**Units and rounding.** Feet, to 0.01 ft (earlier years often to 0.1 ft).
The series metadata gives no vertical datum. The RISE location record lists
EGM96 for Powell and "USGS 1912" for Mead; those fields describe the
location point. Reclamation's 2001 Lake Mead sedimentation survey says
Mead elevations use the dam's 1935 construction survey datum (add 0.55 ft
for NGVD29), and a Reclamation and Park Service dataset puts NAVD88 about
2 ft away. A USGS map service lists Reclamation's Powell elevations as
NGVD29, with NAVD88 about 2.91 ft higher. So the two lakes are not on the
same datum, and neither is on NAVD88. Sources are in `measurement_history`.

**Known quirks.** Daily values are stamped 07:00 UTC, which is midnight
Mountain Standard Time; we use the Mountain day. A RISE text search for
"Lake Mead elevation" returns 8,926 catalog items, most not about Mead, so
the item ids must be pinned.

**Uncertainty.** Not published by RISE.

**License and attribution.** U.S. federal government work, public domain.
Credit: U.S. Bureau of Reclamation.

### National Water Information System, NWIS (U.S. Geological Survey)

**What it is.** Daily mean gage height (parameter 00065, statistic 00003)
for the Missouri River at Bismarck, ND (06342500) and the Ohio River at
Cincinnati, OH (03255000), in feet above the gauge datum.

**Where it comes from.** `https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/dv/`
(tab-separated RDB), and `/nwis/site/` for coordinates and datum. No login.
Both downloads take under 5 seconds. USGS plans to retire this legacy
service in favor of `api.waterdata.usgs.gov`; the script may need to move.

**Version and vintage.** Each value carries an approval code: A (approved)
or P (provisional), with an extra "e" when USGS estimated the value. USGS
revises provisional values until it approves them.

**Coverage.** Bismarck 1897-04-05 to 2026-10-14 (45,755 days); Cincinnati
1987-10-01 to 2026-10-14 (13,302 days). Bismarck's 1920s decade has values
on 74% of days and its 1980s on 93%. Cincinnati is missing 17% of days in
the 1990s. Bismarck has 14 estimated days; Cincinnati none.

**Changes over time.** Bismarck has an unadjusted datum break. USGS
Water-Supply Paper 1309 says the recorder datum was lowered 4.24 ft to the
present datum on October 1, 1934; before that, readings (a chain gauge
2,100 ft upstream until October 17, 1928, then the recorder) were on a
datum 4.24 ft higher. The daily values are not adjusted: the yearly
minimum jumps from -2.9 ft in 1934 to +2.4 ft in 1935. From April 1936 to
September 1937 a wire-weight gauge at a datum 0.47 ft lower was used. Do
not compare Bismarck levels across 1934 without adding 4.24 ft to the
earlier values; our files leave them as published. Garrison Dam, about 75
miles upstream, has regulated flow since November 1953. Cincinnati is held
up at low flow by Markland Locks and Dam (operating from 1963, per the
NWS), before this record starts; the NWS lists four gauge moves at
Cincinnati from 1858 to 1947, all before this file.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None. USGS can publish text
codes instead of numbers (Ice, Eqp, Bkw); none appear in these two series.

**Missing data.** Missing days are absent from the file.

**Revisions.** Provisional values change; we refresh the full series.

**Units and rounding.** Feet to 0.01 ft. Gauge datum 1,619.64 ft NAVD88
(Bismarck; 1,618.28 ft NGVD29 in the 2004 USGS report) and 428.05 ft NAVD88 (Cincinnati) per the USGS site record.

**Known quirks.** The Cincinnati stage record kept by the National Weather
Service and the Corps starts in 1858; the USGS daily file only starts in
1987.

**Uncertainty.** USGS states datum accuracy in the site record (0.01 ft at
Bismarck, 0.05 ft at Cincinnati). Early "daily means" may rest on one or
two staff readings a day.

**License and attribution.** U.S. federal government work, public domain.
Credit: U.S. Geological Survey.

### Water Survey of Canada hydrometric data (Environment and Climate Change Canada)

**What it is.** Daily mean water level in meters above the station datum
for the Fraser River at Shelley (08KB001), Hope (08MF005) and Mission
(08MH024).

**Where it comes from.** Archived daily means (HYDAT) from the GeoMet OGC
API, `https://api.weather.gc.ca/collections/hydrometric-daily-mean/items`.
After HYDAT ends, 5-minute real-time readings from the Water Office
service `https://wateroffice.ec.gc.ca/services/real_time_data/csv/inline`
(about 18 months kept, with an approval column), plus
`hydrometric-realtime` (about 30 days) for any missing timestamps. Station
coordinates and datum from `hydrometric-stations`. No login. About 1.5
minutes for all three stations.

**Version and vintage.** HYDAT is released in batches, so reviewed data lag by months. On October
15, 2026 the daily means ended 2025-10-01 at Shelley, 2025-02-24 at Hope
and 2025-03-04 at Mission.

**Coverage.** Hope water level from 1912; Mission from 1969; Shelley water
level only from 2011 (discharge goes back further, but level does not).
The 1990s have values on only 75% of days at Hope and 58% at Mission. Our
real-time daily means start March 15, 2025, so a gap of about three weeks
remains at Hope (February 25 to March 14, 2025) and ten days at Mission.

**Changes over time.** See `measurement_history` for station moves and
datum changes from WSC station records.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** Rows with discharge and no level are dropped (22,523 at
Shelley, mostly before 2011). WSC symbols: E (estimated, no measured data
that day) is mapped to `estimated`; A (partial day; WSC says the daily mean
was estimated despite gaps in the readings), B (ice conditions), D (dry)
and R (revised) stay `measured` and are counted in each gauge's notes. Only
A and E appear in these level series.

**Revisions.** Real-time data are provisional until WSC finalizes them.
A real-time day is `measured` only when every reading that day is approved
"Final", otherwise `provisional`. We checked our averaging at Shelley, the
one station where the real-time record overlaps HYDAT: on 201 shared days
our daily means matched HYDAT with a median difference of 0.000 m (range
-0.007 to +0.001 m). Hope and Mission have no overlap, so their real-time
datum is assumed, not checked.

**Units and rounding.** Meters to 0.001 m. The API returns float noise
(2.318000078201294); we round to 0.001 m.

**Known quirks.** All three stations use an assumed datum: an arbitrary
local zero, not sea level. Mission is tidal at all flows, so its daily mean
mixes river flow and the tide. A BC government review gives a geodetic
conversion at Mission of +0.073 m before 1987 and +0.043 m after, which
suggests a 0.03 m datum change in 1987 that may not be adjusted. Hope's
channel has built up and cut down over the decades (35 rating curves since
1912), and HYDAT lists Hope as regulated because Kenney Dam on the Nechako
holds back part of the basin. Hope has no level data for 1994 and 1995 and
only 92 days in 1940. The GeoMet API refused connections once during testing
and worked on retry.

**Uncertainty.** We found no published error bars for WSC daily water level.

**License and attribution.** Open Government Licence - Canada. "Contains
information licensed under the Open Government Licence - Canada."

## South America, Australia and the Mekong

Five publishers supply these gauges: Brazil's water agency (ANA), Argentina's
National Water Institute (INA), Paraguay's weather and hydrology office
(DMH-DINAC), Australia's Bureau of Meteorology (BoM) and the Mekong River
Commission (MRC). Fetch scripts: `scripts/fetch/ana.py`, `ina.py`, `dmh.py`,
`bom.py`, `mrc.py` (shared helpers in `sa_asia_common.py`, sourced gauge
histories and reader notes in `sa_asia_history.json`). Each processed gauge
JSON in `data/processed/` carries its own notes, `measurement_history` and
`public_notes`.

Checked October 15, 2026.

### HidroWeb stage series and telemetry (ANA, Brazil)

**What it is.** Daily river stage (water height above a local staff gauge
zero, in cm) for Amazon/Solimoes at Tabatinga, Manacapuru and Obidos, Rio
Negro at Manaus, and the Paraguay River at Ladario. Each row in our file is
one day.

**Where it comes from.** ANA's SOAP web service
`https://telemetriaws1.ana.gov.br/ServiceANA.asmx`, no login, XML.
`HidroSerieHistorica?codEstacao=<code>&tipoDados=1` returns the whole stage
history in one call (2 to 11 MB). Each row is one month with columns
`Cota01..Cota31` and a status per day (`Cota01Status..`). Months can appear
up to three times per consistency level: one row of daily means
(`MediaDiaria=1`, time 00:00) and one row per reading hour (07:00 and 17:00).
`DadosHidrometeorologicos` returns telemetry (mostly 15-minute readings) for a
date range; we ask one calendar year at a time (about 5 to 8 seconds each).
`HidroInventario` gives coordinates and station periods.

**Version and vintage.** No version number. Each month carries a consistency
level: 1 = raw ("bruto"), 2 = consistent, meaning reviewed by ANA
("consistido"). On October 15, 2026, level 2 ran to December 2023 for
Tabatinga, Manacapuru, Obidos and Ladario, and to December 2014 for Manaus,
whose historic file stops there. Level 1 runs to early or mid 2026. When ANA
reviews a period, level 2 values appear next to the level 1 values; we refresh
the whole series each time.

**Coverage.** Manaus 1902, Ladario 1900, Manacapuru 1972, Obidos 1968,
Tabatinga 1982, all to October 2026. Almost every day has a value
(`coverage_by_decade`). Obidos has no data for 1969. Our rule: level 2 value
if it exists for that day, else level 1 (marked `provisional`). After the
historic file ends we add the telemetry daily mean (`provisional`). ANA flags
individual days as estimated with status 2; we mark those `estimated`. The
share is small except Manacapuru in the 1990s (13%) and Tabatinga in the
2000s (7%). ANA's guidance on consistency says doubtful values are marked and
filled values are marked, and that daily means should preferably come from 24
hourly readings; for these staff gauges the daily value is the mean of the
07:00 and 17:00 readings. ANA does not say how it fills an estimated day.

**Changes over time.** Manaus switches from the reviewed staff gauge series to
telemetry on January 1, 2015. On 2,274 days in 2005 to 2014 that have both,
telemetry minus staff gauge has a median of 0 cm, and every year's median is
between -1.2 and 0 cm, so the join is continuous. Telemetry at the other four
gauges agrees with the raw staff series to within 1 cm on a typical day. At
Obidos, an older 1927 to 1948 gauge used a different zero (likely 77 cm
higher); our file starts with the current gauge in 1968.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None. Negative values are real
readings below the gauge zero (Tabatinga, Obidos, Ladario).

**Missing data.** Missing days are blank in the monthly rows. No fill values
(such as 99999) were found. Per-day status codes: 0 blank, 1 real, 2
estimated, 3 doubtful, 4 dry staff gauge. This legend comes from HidroWeb
export headers as quoted by third-party tools, not from an ANA page we could
read. Codes 3 and 4 appear on up to a few dozen raw days per gauge (235 doubtful days at Ladario); we keep those values as
published.

**Revisions.** Level 1 values are replaced by level 2 values after review,
usually a year or more later. Telemetry can be revised after field visits that
re-level the gauges (SGB bulletin, 2017).

**Units and rounding.** Whole centimeters in the historic series. Telemetry
daily means are rounded to 0.1 cm.

**Known quirks.** The XML schema root is named `http://MRCS/`. Duplicate month
rows (daily-mean row plus per-hour rows) must be collapsed, or every day
appears two or three times. Tabatinga has several one-day jumps of 3 to 4 m
in 1989 to 1992 (for example 990 cm for three days in July 1992) that look
like transcription errors; kept as published. The Manaus gauge zero is a
local reference; SGB says to subtract 7.00 m to refer a reading to sea level.

**Uncertainty.** ANA publishes no error bars.

**License and attribution.** No license on the service itself. The SNIRH
metadata record for the national network allows reproduction of the data
provided the source is cited
(https://metadados.snirh.gov.br/geonetwork/srv/api/records/f85dbf06-a869-414c-afc5-bb01869e9156).
Credit: "Agencia Nacional de Aguas e Saneamento Basico (ANA), HidroWeb."

### Alert system a5 API, Prefectura gauges (INA, Argentina)

**What it is.** Daily stage of the Parana River in meters above each gauge's
zero at Posadas, Corrientes, Parana and Rosario. The staff gauges belong to
the Prefectura Naval Argentina (coast guard); INA hosts the series.

**Where it comes from.** `https://alerta.ina.gob.ar/a5/obs/puntual/series/<id>/observaciones?timestart=...&timeend=...`,
JSON, no token. Series metadata, coordinates and gauge zero from
`/a5/obs/puntual/series?estacion_id=<id>&var_id=2`. We request 20-year
chunks; a full gauge takes about 15 seconds.

**Version and vintage.** No version. Values update daily; the last value on
October 15, 2026 was October 14.

**Coverage.** Rosario 1884, Posadas and Corrientes 1901, Parana 1902, all to
October 2026. Most decades have a value on 98% to 100% of days; Parana's
first decade has 90%. INA publishes no flag for filled or estimated values,
so everything is marked `measured`.

**Changes over time.** Before 2014 to 2016 there is one value a day. After
that some days have two to eight readings (Rosario 1,285 days, Corrientes
1,793); we average them and round the mean to 0.01 m, the precision INA
publishes, so `daily_stat` is "mixed". A mean that ends in exactly 5 mm
(such as 5.255 m) can round either way, because computers do not store such
decimals exactly; the difference is never more than 0.01 m. Timestamps are 07:00
local through the 1970s, 06:00 in the 1980s, 04:00 in the 2000s and 00:00
in the 2020s. The uniform early hour looks like a loading convention, not a
documented reading hour. Yacyreta dam, downstream of Posadas, has held a
reservoir there since 1994, raised in steps to 83 m by February 2011, so
Posadas levels before and after are not comparable.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None found.

**Missing data.** Missing days have no row. No fill values found.

**Revisions.** INA can overwrite values (each observation has a
`timeupdate`); we refresh the full series.

**Units and rounding.** Meters to two decimals (1 cm). Daily means of
several readings are rounded back to two decimals (changed October 17,
2026; before that they were stored to 0.001 m, finer than the gauge is
read).

**Known quirks.** Gauge zeros are tied to different national reference systems
(IGN, IGM, MOP). INA's 2024 report says Corrientes' zero is uncertain (41.95,
42.01 or 42.39 m depending on the document). Posadas has no published zero.
Rosario's early record was affected by a gradually sinking gauge; Antico et
al. (2020) corrected it, and it is not stated whether INA's copy includes the
correction. INA does have a station for Asuncion (station 157, DMH-DINAC
network), but its series is not public, so Asuncion comes from DMH. INA's
nearest public long Paraguay River series is Puerto Pilcomayo (series 55,
1910 on), across the river from Asuncion; we did not use it.

**Uncertainty.** Not published.

**License and attribution.** No license, terms of use or citation statement
found on the a5 site or its README. Credit "Instituto Nacional del Agua (INA)
and Prefectura Naval Argentina." Clear reuse terms before publishing.

### Nivel del rio, Asuncion web table (DMH-DINAC, Paraguay)

**What it is.** One daily level ("nivel del dia") for the Paraguay River at
Asuncion, in meters above the gauge zero.

**Where it comes from.** HTML page
`https://www.meteorologia.gov.py/nivel-rio/vermas_convencional.php?code=2000086218&page=N`,
15 days per page, newest first, roughly 3,000 pages back to 1904. No API or
download. The page's date-range form is commented out and the `desde`/`hasta`
parameters return no rows. We cache every page in `data/raw/dmh/` and the
script resumes where it stopped.

**Version and vintage.** No version. Page boundaries shift by one row each day
as new values are added at the top; the script drops identical repeats and
fails on a date with two different values.

**Coverage.** January 1, 1904 to October 15, 2026: 2,988 pages with data (page 2,989 is
empty), 44,815 days. Every decade has a value on more than 99% of days. No
quality flags; every value is `measured`.

**Changes over time.** Not documented.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None. Values below zero are real
(for example -1.02 m in September 2024).

**Missing data.** Not documented; gaps show up as missing dates.

**Revisions.** Unknown.

**Units and rounding.** Meters to two decimals, printed with an "m" suffix.

**Known quirks.** With curl each page past page 1 took about 77 seconds to
answer; with a Python requests session (keeping the cookie from page 1) each
took about 1 second, so the full scrape took about 90 minutes over two runs.
`--refresh` re-downloads only page 1; older pages are treated as fixed. Coordinates are not on the page; we use the ones INA lists for the
same DMH station code.

**Uncertainty.** Not published.

**License and attribution.** None stated. Credit "Direccion de Meteorologia
e Hidrologia (DMH-DINAC), Paraguay." Clear reuse terms before publishing.

### Water Data Online, KiWIS API (Bureau of Meteorology, Australia)

**What it is.** Daily mean water course level for the Murray at Albury, Swan
Hill and Euston and the Darling at Bourke, in meters.

**Where it comes from.**
`https://www.bom.gov.au/waterdata/services?service=kisters&type=queryServices&datasource=0&format=json&request=...`.
No login, but BoM rejects requests without a browser-like User-Agent.
`getTimeseriesList` finds the series `DMQaQc.Merged.DailyMean.24HR` for
parameter "Water Course Level"; `getTimeseriesValues` with
`returnfields=Timestamp,Value,Quality Code` returns the whole record in one
call (0.3 to 2.3 MB). `getStationList` gives coordinates and the data owner.

**Version and vintage.** No version. "DMQaQc.Merged" is not defined on any
BoM page we found; BoM's Water Data Online info sheet says the published
series combine validated data and provisional raw data, so this is most
likely that combined series. Data owners (WaterNSW for Albury and Bourke;
Victoria's Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action for Swan Hill
and Euston) can revise history.

**Coverage.** Albury 1885, Swan Hill 1974, Euston 1975, Bourke 1895 (one year)
then 1972, all to October 13, 2026. Quality codes, from `getQualityCodes`
and BoM's WDTF quality code list: 10 = A "best available", 90 = B
"compromised", 110 = C "estimate", 140 = E "unknown", 210 = F "missing or
incorrect". We map A and B to `measured`, C to `estimated`, and drop F.
E is `measured` before 1991 and `provisional` from 1991 on (changed October
17, 2026). Before 1991 E covers the once-daily record. After that it comes
back on recent days that have not been checked: Albury 76 days (2 in 1998,
8 in 2015, 12 in 2017, 54 in 2026), Bourke 190 days (120 from June 16 to
October 13, 2026, one run of 64 days from October 11 to December 13, 1995,
and 6 single days from 2011 to 2022). Swan Hill and Euston have none. Its
share per decade is still reported in each gauge's notes. Estimated (C) values
are 27% of Swan Hill's and 32% of Euston's days in the 1970s, and a few
percent after that. Albury's values are nearly all E before 1990; Bourke's
are E from 1972 to 1988. BoM's reference station documentation maps NSW's
"daily, accumulated or spot data" code to E and calls its NSW mappings
"provisional only," so E there likely means a once-daily manual reading
rather than a bad value.

**Changes over time.** Albury has one value a day through 1975 (mostly
09:00), then several a day from 1976, consistent with a recorder. Swan Hill
and Euston start with several readings a day. Bourke has nothing from 1896 to
mid-1972. Dams and weirs: Hume Dam (1936, enlarged 1961) and Dartmouth Dam
(1979) upstream of Albury; Euston Weir (1937) at Euston; Torrumbarry Weir
(1924) upstream of Swan Hill; Bourke weir (1897, rebuilt 1941).

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None. No F-coded values in these
four series. Timestamps with no value (code -1) are dropped: 28,156 at
Bourke, mostly the 1896 to 1972 gap.

**Missing data.** Missing days appear as a null value with code -1.

**Revisions.** Recent values can be provisional and replaced; we refresh the
full series.

**Units and rounding.** Meters to three decimals. Albury, Swan Hill and
Bourke are local gauge heights. Euston values are water surface elevations
(42 to 52 m), most likely meters above the Australian Height Datum, which BoM
does not state.

**Known quirks.** Station 414203 (Euston) has two station entries about 2.7 km
apart; the daily mean series is on station_id 2877981. Some station lists
return blank coordinates for one of a pair.

**Uncertainty.** Not published beyond the quality codes.

**License and attribution.** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
(https://www.bom.gov.au/water/about/waterDataOnline/); the Water Data Online
footer still says "Creative Commons Attribution Australia." BoM asks users to
check the data owners' preferences. Credit "Bureau of Meteorology, Water Data
Online; data from WaterNSW / Victorian Department of Energy, Environment and
Climate Action."

### Time-series API (Mekong River Commission)

**Not shown on the page.** Left out from October 19, 2026 because MRC's
license requires written permission to republish (see "Sources that need
permission are left out" in the rules above). The section stays as the
record of what was fetched and how.

**What it is.** Water level in meters above each gauge's zero at Chiang Saen
(Thailand), Vientiane (Laos), Pakse (Laos) and Kratie (Cambodia), from the
"Water Level.Manual" series (staff gauge, read one to six times a day).

**Where it comes from.** Inventory:
`https://timeseries.api.mrcmekong.org/api/v1/ts/inventory/timeSeriesList`
(1.1 MB JSON, coordinates included). Data:
`.../ts/data/timeSeriesCorrectedData/<uniqueId>?sd=...&ed=...`, one call per
series (2 to 4 MB). No login for the API; the MRC portal needs a free account
to download.

**Version and vintage.** AQUARIUS system, approval level "Working" for the
whole record of all four series (not formally approved). Updated daily.

**Coverage.** Vientiane 1923, Kratie 1933, Pakse 1960, Chiang Saen 1960, all
to October 15, 2026. Vientiane is missing 1940 to 1941, 1945 to 1946 and 1953
to 1959. About 7,000 days per gauge have more than one reading; we use the
daily mean. There is no per-point quality flag. The API does carry Grades and
Notes intervals. We mark as `estimated` any interval whose note says the data
were filled, estimated or interpolated, and grade 41 intervals (MRC used
grade 41 for the November 2024 gaps filled from telemetry at Chiang Saen and
Kratie). At Kratie we also mark 1940, 1957 and 1975 to 1979 as estimated: MRC's
2005 data inventory had no Kratie water level for those years, and the API now
has complete values with no explanation. Their day-to-day variability looks
like the years around them, so we cannot tell filled days from real ones.

**Changes over time.** Chiang Saen moved on December 15, 1993; Cochrane et al.
(2014) say a 0.62 m correction is needed to compare later and earlier values.
The API series jumps about +0.45 m overnight on December 16, 1993, in the dry
season, so it appears not corrected. We publish it as is and flag it. Chinese
dams upstream (Manwan filled 1992, Xiaowan 2010, Nuozhadu 2014) changed dry
and wet season levels at Chiang Saen and below.

**Suppressed, censored or masked values.** None.

**Missing data.** Missing days have no point. We drop isolated 0.00 values
that sit more than 1 m from both neighboring days (two at Chiang Saen, one at
Pakse, two at Kratie); these are placeholders, not readings.

**Revisions.** MRC edits history (the Notes record typo fixes and a 2022
replacement of Pakse data with backup manual records). We refresh in full.

**Units and rounding.** Meters. Values come with long float tails (for example
6.576172678319064), likely from a datum conversion; we round to 1 mm. Gauge
zeros from MRC's 2006 flood report: Chiang Saen 357.1 m, Vientiane 158.0 m,
Pakse 86.5 m, Kratie -1.08 m above mean sea level (reference not named there;
JICA gives Ko Lak datum for Vientiane).

**Known quirks.** Grade codes 11, 21 and 31 appear on short intervals whose
notes describe typo fixes or checks against neighbor gauges; MRC does not
publish what the codes mean. Sources disagree on Vientiane's start (1913 in a
2008 flood report, 1923 in the inventory and API).

**Uncertainty.** The API summary says "The accuracy and reliability of this
data is not guaranteed."

**License and attribution.** Not open. MRC's 2005 Non-commercial Data Use
Licence allows non-commercial use only and no redistribution of data or
derived products without written permission
(https://archive.iwlearn.org/mrcmekong.org/download/spatial/nonCommmercialDataUseLicense.pdf).
No current license text was found on the portal. Get MRC permission before
publishing charts built from these values. Credit "Mekong River Commission."
