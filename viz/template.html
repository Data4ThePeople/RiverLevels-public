Each blue river on the map has at least one gauge with a long daily record of its water level. Click a river or choose one from the list. Where a river is drawn blue beside a dashed orange line, at least one of its gauges also has a record of how much water flows past it, and the Flow switch above the chart shows it. Dashed orange rivers have no open water level record but do have a long daily flow record. Gray rivers are major rivers whose history is not openly published.

Before you look: old readings are less certain

Many early readings were taken by hand from a marked post in the river, often once a day. Since then, many gauges have moved, had their zero point reset, or been affected by new dams and by riverbeds that wore down over time. Some records have years with missing days, and a few have values the publisher filled in.

Flow is almost never measured every day. Publishers compute it from the water level with a rating curve that is checked a few times a year, so the highest and lowest flows are the least certain.

For every gauge we show how complete the record is year by year, mark readings the publisher estimated or has not yet checked, and list what we know about how the gauge changed. Compare old and new readings with care.
